# Home Evaluation Rubric

Use this checklist for every home evaluation. Keep the final brief decision-ready, not encyclopedic.

## Required Sections

### Property Snapshot

- Address, neighborhood, city, ZIP.
- List price, price changes, days on market, listing status, source date.
- Beds/baths, square footage, lot size, year built/remodeled, parking, HOA, taxes.
- Property type and notable layout features.

### Schools

- Identify assigned elementary, middle, and high schools from official district/boundary sources when possible.
- Summarize ratings from multiple sources only as secondary signals.
- Check fit factors: school level, commute, program notes, boundary uncertainty, future reassignment risk.
- Flag if the home is in a desirable district but the assigned school is less aligned with expectations.

### Safety

- Use official police dashboards or crime maps when possible.
- Summarize violent crime vs property crime separately.
- Identify practical residential risks: vehicle prowls, package theft, burglary, traffic/street racing, nearby transit/commercial corridors.
- Avoid overclaiming from neighborhood reputation or third-party crime grades.

### Area Value And Appreciation

- Evaluate the most relevant geography: immediate neighborhood first, ZIP/city second.
- Compare 5-year appreciation using current reliable sources, noting whether data is sale-price median, typical home value index, assessed value, or ACS owner-occupied value.
- Separate long-term appreciation from recent year-over-year cooling or depreciation.
- Note sample-size problems for small neighborhoods.

### Property Value And Comps

- Compare list price against nearby recent sales with similar square footage, beds/baths, age, lot, condition, view, school assignment, and renovation level.
- Calculate price per square foot and compare to relevant comps.
- Watch for mix issues: new construction vs older homes, basement ADU/in-law setup, large lots, steep/slope lots, odd layouts.
- State whether list price appears low, fair, stretched, or unsupported.

### Negotiation Leverage

- Check days on market, price reductions, relist history, offer deadline, buyer activity, sale-to-list ratio, inventory, and recent comparable concessions.
- Consider seller motivation signals from emails, agent notes, stale listing, vacant home, failed pending sale, or large price cuts.
- Recommend posture: compete hard, offer near list, seek concession, inspect-first, wait, or pass.
- Include suggested questions for buyer agent.

### Condition And Inspection Risk

- Review seller disclosure, inspection reports, permits, listing photos, age of roof/HVAC/water heater, drainage, sewer/septic, foundation, slope, remodel quality, and additions.
- Flag specialist inspections: sewer scope, roof, structural, drainage/geotech, pest, electrical, HVAC, chimney/fireplace, arborist, pool/spa.
- Distinguish cosmetic issues from expensive system risks.

### Title, HOA, Permits, And Legal

- Review preliminary title, Schedule B exceptions, easements, CCRs, HOA documents, shared maintenance agreements, encroachments, permits, open code issues, and special assessments.
- Flag anything affecting use, remodeling, access, resale, financing, or insurance.
- Recommend title officer / real estate attorney review when exceptions are unclear.

### Financial Fit

- Estimate monthly cost drivers: mortgage, taxes, insurance, HOA, utilities, maintenance reserve.
- Identify likely near-term capital expenses.
- Compare rent-vs-buy only if user asks or the situation calls for it.
- Consider liquidity/resale risk if the home is unusual or highly priced for the pocket.

### Lifestyle Fit

- Commute, transit, road noise, freeway proximity, airport/flight path if relevant.
- Neighborhood feel, walkability, parks, shopping, schools, privacy, yard usability.
- Fit for family plans, guests, remote work, multigenerational living, storage, and future resale.

### Climate And Physical Site Risks

- Check flood, wildfire/smoke exposure, landslide/slope, drainage, tree risk, heat/cooling needs, and insurance availability where relevant.
- Use official FEMA/city/county maps when possible.

### Open Questions And Next Actions

- List the highest-value unknowns.
- Recommend the next concrete action: ask agent, request documents, run comps, tour, inspect, write offer, or pass.
- Save open loops in the property note.
